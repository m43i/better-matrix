# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

Better Matrix welcomes contributors of every background and experience level. We want everyone
to be able to participate without harassment, regardless of age, disability, ethnicity, gender
identity or expression, nationality, appearance, race, religion, or sexual orientation.

Everyone participating in the project, including maintainers, is expected to follow this code.

## Expected behaviour

- Treat people with respect, empathy, and patience.
- Welcome newcomers and help them learn.
- Give constructive feedback and discuss ideas without attacking people.
- Respect different experiences, viewpoints, and boundaries.
- Take responsibility for mistakes, apologise when appropriate, and learn from feedback.
- Keep discussions relevant and consider what is best for the community.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, discrimination, slurs, or demeaning comments.
- Threats, intimidation, stalking, or encouragement of violence.
- Sexualised language or imagery and unwelcome sexual attention or advances.
- Trolling, personal attacks, deliberate disruption, or repeated unwanted contact.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Retaliation against anyone who reports an incident or helps address one.
- Other conduct that would reasonably make participation unsafe or hostile.

## Scope

This code applies in project spaces, including issues, pull requests, code reviews, discussions,
and project chat rooms. It also applies when someone represents Better Matrix in public, such
as at an event or through an official project account.

## Reporting

Report incidents privately to the project maintainer, Malte Eiting, on Matrix at
[@m43i:m43i.dev](https://matrix.to/#/@m43i:m43i.dev). Include what happened, where and when it
happened, and any relevant links or screenshots you feel comfortable sharing. You can report
something you experienced or witnessed.

Do not post sensitive reports in public issues. Reports will be reviewed fairly and handled as
confidentially as possible. Information will only be shared when needed to investigate or
respond to the incident. A person involved in a report should not decide its outcome; where
possible, an uninvolved maintainer will handle it.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for explaining and enforcing this code consistently and fairly.
They may edit or remove content that violates it and restrict participation when needed.

Responses depend on the severity, impact, and pattern of behaviour. They may include:

1. A private correction explaining the problem and the expected change.
2. A warning with clear boundaries and consequences for further violations.
3. A temporary ban from project spaces for serious or repeated violations.
4. A permanent ban for sustained harassment, threats, or other severe misconduct.

Serious incidents may warrant an immediate ban. Maintainers will explain their decisions to
the people affected when appropriate, while protecting the privacy of those involved.
